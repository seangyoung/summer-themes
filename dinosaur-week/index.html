Dinosaur Week

A balanced week of fossils, footprints, prehistoric creatures, library finds, water play, museum time, and one big dino-filled finale. This version starts on Sunday, keeps Monday as library day, and mixes cool indoor days with a few strong outdoor moments.

Budget Shape Mostly low-cost at home, with one paid field trip and one optional extra.
Outdoor Balance Three outdoor days, three indoor/cool-down days, one flexible field trip day.
Best Ages Built for ages 6 to 16 with room for younger siblings to join the fun.
Big Hooks Fossil digs, frozen-egg rescues, track detectives, museum dinosaurs, movie night, and dino snacks.

Theme Overview

Good background links for the week: Dinosaurs, Paleontology, Fossils, Tyrannosaurus, Triceratops.

How This Week Holds Together

  • Core ideas: dinosaurs, fossils, footprints, paleontologists, habitats, eggs, skeletons, and prehistoric landscapes.
  • Monday stays reserved for the library so the rest of the week can pull from a fresh book haul.
  • Outdoor pieces are used where they make the week feel bigger: digging, water play, and habitat building.
  • Indoor days help with Texas heat and give space for crafts, movies, and science-heavy activities without turning the week into school.

Planning Notes

  • Home-day supply estimates assume about 8 people and that you already have basics like tape, glue, markers, bowls, and baking sheets.
  • Pool, splash pad, parks, and the rocky beach area are treated as available free resources.
  • Fee-based outings include direct official links.
  • Field Trip Day lands on Friday here, but this format can shift to any non-Sunday day later.

Main Paid Outing

Perot Museum of Nature and Science. Plan roughly $20 to $30 per person for timed museum admission depending on age and date.

Optional Extra

Dinosaur Valley State Park. Official entrance fee: $8 ages 13+, free for ages 12 and under.

Overall Budget Feel

Most home days land around $1 to $5 per person, with the main outing as the big-ticket item and Dinosaur Valley as a very strong optional add-on.

Weekly Plan

Sunday through Saturday, with named days and a better heat-friendly indoor/outdoor rhythm.

Sunday

Fossil Hunt Kickoff | Outdoor morning, indoor evening

$2 to $4 per person

Main Activity

Set up a backyard dig site with buried toy dinosaurs, shells, bones, and “fossils” in sand or dirt. Give everyone brushes, spoons, and notebooks to record their finds.

Craft

Make salt-dough fossils and press in toy dinosaur feet, shells, leaves, or small plastic bones.

Food

Dirt pudding cups, fossil crackers, and easy grilled hot dogs or dinosaur nuggets for dinner.

Entertainment Pick

Start with The Land Before Time (1988, G) or one episode of Prehistoric Planet.

Monday

Library Expedition | Indoor anchor day

$1 to $3 per person

Main Activity

Visit the library and let everyone choose at least one dinosaur title, one picture-heavy nonfiction book, and one “just for fun” pick. Build a weekly stack for read-alouds and quiet time.

Craft

Make dinosaur bookmarks or a “field journal” with tea-stained pages, a hand-drawn title page, and space for facts and sketches all week.

Food

Dino egg muffins, apple slices, and trail mix in a tote bag for a simple library picnic before or after the visit.

Reading Focus

Great library targets: Encyclopedia Prehistorica Dinosaurs, The Dinosaurs of Waterhouse Hawkins, Ultimate Dinopedia.

Tuesday

Frozen Dino Egg Rescue | Outdoor patio activity with optional water play

$0 to $2 per person

Main Activity

The night before, freeze small plastic dinosaurs inside water-filled balloons. On the driveway, front sidewalk, or back patio, peel away the balloons and let the kids free the dinosaurs with warm-water squeeze bottles, eyedroppers, salt, and small cups. Use a shallow plastic tray to contain the meltwater.

Water Play Add-On

After the egg rescue, cool off at the splash pad, play in the hose, or have a regular pool swim. Keep this separate from the frozen-egg activity so the outing stays simple.

Food

Watermelon, popsicles, crackers, and sandwiches. Keep this one easy and cold.

Show Pick

Good lighter options: Dino Dana or Dino Dan.

Wednesday

Dinosaur Track Detectives | Indoor cool-down day

$1 to $3 per person

Main Activity

Press toy dinosaur feet into play dough or salt dough, then compare the tracks with rulers and magnifying glasses. Have everyone sketch a favorite print in a field notebook and guess which toy dinosaur made each trail.

Craft

Make a paper “trackway” by stamping or tracing prints across kraft paper, then label the likely dinosaur, the direction of travel, and a made-up discovery-site name.

Food

Bone-shaped pretzels, “amber” gelatin cups, and a simple pasta lunch.

Documentary Pick

Older kids and teens: Walking with Dinosaurs or Planet Dinosaur.

Thursday

Prehistoric Shoreline | Outdoor adventure day

$1 to $3 per person

Main Activity

Use the rocky beach area to build mini prehistoric habitats out of stones, driftwood, ferns, and shallow water channels. Add toy dinosaurs and invent which creatures lived where.

Nature Tie-In

Look for textures, footprints, shells, and rock layers that make the place feel like a “real” dig or ancient shoreline without overcomplicating it.

Food

Picnic lunch, fruit, and a cooler of drinks. This is a good day for chips, wraps, and cookies.

Book Pairing

Bring A Dinosaur Named Sue or DK Eyewitness Dinosaur for shoreline reading breaks.

Friday

Field Trip Day | Indoor museum day

$20 to $30 per person

Main Outing

Go to the Perot Museum of Nature and Science for the dinosaur-heavy museum experience, cooler temperatures, and a true anchor outing for the week.

What To Do There

  • Let everyone choose one favorite skeleton and one weirdest prehistoric creature.
  • Take quick notes or sketches for Saturday’s home “museum.”
  • Keep food costs down by planning a packed snack or meal outside the museum.

Optional Extra

If you want the more literal dinosaur-track outing, use Dinosaur Valley State Park as an add-on or substitute. Official fee: $8 for ages 13 and up, free for ages 12 and under.

Movie Pairing

This is the best day for Jurassic Park (1993, PG-13) if the older kids want it, or Dinosaur (2000, PG) for a broader family pick.

Saturday

Jurassic Celebration | Indoor finale with flexible extras

$2 to $5 per person

Main Activity

Turn the house into a family dino museum. Display fossils, tracks, field journals, skeleton builds, and habitat scenes from the week.

Craft And Game

Make final exhibit labels, award funny categories like “Best Bite” and “Top Paleontologist,” and finish with dinosaur trivia.

Food

Fossil cookies, popcorn, dinosaur cake, fruit, and make-your-own mini pizzas or a big easy snack board.

Final Entertainment

Finish with The Good Dinosaur (2015, PG), Walking with Dinosaurs (2013, PG), or a favorite returning episode from the week.

Books, Shows, And Movies

More specific and more usable, with direct links for quick browsing and holds.

Books

Ages 6 to 8: Read-Alouds And Early Readers

  • Encyclopedia Prehistorica Dinosaurs by Robert Sabuda and Matthew Reinhart
  • The Dinosaurs of Waterhouse Hawkins by Barbara Kerley
  • Dinosaurs Before Dark by Mary Pope Osborne
  • A Dinosaur Named Sue by Fay Robinson
  • National Geographic Little Kids First Big Book of Dinosaurs by Catherine D. Hughes

Ages 8 to 12: Independent Reading And Visual Reference

  • Ultimate Dinopedia by Don Lessem
  • DK Eyewitness Dinosaur
  • Fossil by Fossil by Sara Levine

Ages 11 to 16: Paleontology And History

  • Bone Wars by Kathleen V. Kudlinski
  • The Rise and Fall of the Dinosaurs by Steve Brusatte
