Space Week

A week of rockets, moons, planets, galaxies, astronomy, NASA missions, and just enough sci-fi to keep the whole crew interested. The plan mixes hands-on science with imaginative play and uses outdoor time where it is most rewarding.

Budget ShapeMostly low-cost home days, one moderate field trip, and one optional bigger upgrade.
Outdoor BalanceRocket launches and a star party are outdoors; the hottest parts of the week stay inside.
Best AgesBuilt for ages 6 to 16, from crafts and games to design challenges and real astronomy.
Big HooksMission patches, straw rockets, a Moon base, galaxy art, a planetarium, and a backyard skywatch.

Theme Overview

Useful background links: NASA Space Place, NASA Kids’ Club, space exploration, the Solar System, and astronomy.

How This Week Holds Together

  • Core ideas: human spaceflight, rockets, planets, the Moon, galaxies, telescopes, and the night sky.
  • The sequence moves from a family mission and library research into designing, launching, building, and finally observing.
  • Monday is a library visit; Friday is the main DFW field trip; Saturday closes with backyard astronomy and a movie.

Planning Notes

  • Home-day estimates assume about eight people and basic supplies such as tape, scissors, markers, bowls, and a television.
  • Do outdoor activities early in the day or near sunset. The pool or splash pad is presented as a simple optional cooldown, not a themed attraction.
  • Public-show schedules change, so check the field-trip links before choosing a date.

Main Field Trip

UTA Planetarium public shows in Arlington. General admission is $10; children over 2 and seniors are $8. Plan about $9 to $12 per person once shared parking is included.

Optional Bigger Upgrade

Frontiers of Flight Museum at Love Field has aviation and space-flight galleries plus free parking. Standard admission is $20 ages 13+ and $12 ages 3–12.

Low-Cost Backbone

NASA has free, printable activities for Moon habitats, planet masks, galaxy art, rockets, moon phases, and more through NASA Space Place PDFs.

Weekly Plan

Sunday through Saturday, with a practical DFW summer rhythm.

Sunday

Mission Control Kickoff | Indoor launch day

$1 to $3 per person

Main Activity

Choose a crew name, make a simple mission board, and give each person a role such as commander, engineer, science officer, navigator, or flight surgeon. Pick two or three “missions” to complete during the week.

Craft

Create astronaut badges or mission patches with cardstock, foil, markers, string, and stickers. Older kids can design a crew logo and a short mission motto.

Food

Rocket dogs, star-shaped fruit, blue punch, and popcorn “meteorites.”

Entertainment Pick

Open with a short family browse through NASA Space Place and choose one fact each person wants to investigate this week.

Monday

Library Launch | Indoor anchor day

$1 to $3 per person

Main Activity

Visit the Flower Mound Public Library and let each person choose one space book, one nonfiction or visual-reference book, and one just-for-fun pick. Build a shared stack for read-alouds and quiet time.

Craft

Make galaxy bookmarks using black cardstock, white paint pen stars, and colorful paper scraps. Start a weekly “mission log” for sketches, facts, and questions.

Food

Planet sandwiches or wraps, orange slices, and trail mix in a tote for a simple library picnic.

Reading Focus

Look for Mousetronaut, A Rover’s Story, Team Moon, and Hidden Figures: Young Readers’ Edition.

Tuesday

Backyard Rocket Range | Outdoor morning

$1 to $3 per person

Main Activity

Make light paper rockets that fit over drinking straws, then hold a launch-distance contest in a shady yard or driveway. Change one feature at a time—nose shape, fin size, or body length—and see what flies farthest.

Craft

Decorate each rocket for a different destination: Moon, Mars, Jupiter, an imaginary exoplanet, or a space station. Older kids can keep a simple launch-data chart.

Food

Cheese sticks as “fuel rods,” fruit skewers, and frozen grapes or popsicles after the outdoor session.

Optional Extension

Use NASA’s bubble-powered rocket activity for another at-home launch challenge.

Wednesday

Moon Base Builders | Indoor cool-down day

$2 to $5 per person

Main Activity

Build a Moon habitat from cardboard boxes, foil, paper tubes, recyclables, and small figures. Every base needs a sleeping space, food supply, power source, rover route, and a way to protect its crew.

Craft

Use the printable NASA Moon Habitat activity as a starting point, then add homemade modules and a crumpled-paper Moon surface.

Food

Oreo Moon phases, quesadillas cut into crescents, and silver-wrapped “astronaut” snacks.

Screen Pick

Try a family episode of The Magic School Bus focused on the solar system, then have everyone explain one feature of their Moon base.

Thursday

Galaxy Studio | Indoor art with optional water cooldown

$1 to $4 per person

Main Activity

Make coffee-filter galaxies with washable markers and a light spray of water, then mount the dried art on black paper. Add a few white paint-pen stars and make each galaxy’s name and discovery story.

Outdoor Add-On

Use the pool, splash pad, or hose as a straightforward late-afternoon cooldown. Keep the art inside and let the water play simply break up a hot day.

Food

Planet pizzas, blue gelatin “Neptune cups,” and a bowl of popcorn “asteroids.”

NASA Link

NASA offers a related GALEX galaxy montage activity for a different art approach.

Friday

Field Trip Day | Indoor planetarium outing

$9 to $12 per person

Main Outing

Go to a public show at the UTA Planetarium in Arlington. Public shows include a feature presentation, a Texas night-sky tour, Q&A, and often a virtual roller-coaster finale. Check the rotating schedule before choosing a date.

Cost Notes

Current public-show tickets are $10 general admission and $8 for children over 2 and seniors. The nearby Maverick Garage is listed at $4 per hour, up to $14 per day; split that across the group or look for weekend metered parking details on the visitor page.

Optional Bigger Upgrade

For a longer aviation-and-space day, visit the Frontiers of Flight Museum at Love Field. Admission is $20 ages 13+, $12 ages 3–12, and parking is free.

Bring Along

Pack a simple snack and a few mission-log pages. Ask each person to draw or write down the one thing they want to look for in the real night sky on Saturday.

Saturday

Star Party Finale | Outdoor evening

$1 to $4 per person

Main Activity

Set up blankets, a telescope or binoculars if available, and a simple star map. Start after sunset with the Moon or bright planets, then look for a few constellations. NASA’s skywatching tips are an easy guide.

Craft And Game

Make flashlight constellation cards by poking holes in black paper, then hold them over a flashlight beam. Finish with family space trivia or mission-log awards.

Food

Popcorn, s’mores or campfire cones, and star cookies. Keep a cooler of drinks outside for a low-effort finish.

Movie Night

Choose WALL-E (2008, G) for a broad family pick, or Apollo 13 (1995, PG) for older kids and teens.
